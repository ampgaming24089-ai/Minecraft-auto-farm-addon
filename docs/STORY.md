# The Hollow Veil — Story & Lore

## The Premise

Long before the Overworld had a name, there was a third place: **the Veil**, a
thin grey country stitched between the world of the living and the true
afterlife. The dead did not linger there — they only *passed through* it, for
a night, guided by three Wardens who kept the crossing gentle:

- **The Hollow King**, who carried the memory of the dead so grief would not
  follow them into the beyond.
- **The Weeping Widow**, who sang the dead to sleep so the crossing didn't hurt.
- **Malacoda**, once a Warden of thresholds and locks, who guarded the one
  door that was never, ever supposed to open.

Malacoda opened it anyway. Something on the other side wanted out badly
enough to make a very old, very lonely guardian believe it would be kind to
the world. It was not. The instant the door cracked, whatever came through
unmade Malacoda first — burned the Warden down into a demon wearing its
shape — and the corruption spread outward through the Veil like frost across
glass. The Hollow King's memories turned to hoarding and rage. The Weeping
Widow's lullabies turned to screams that shatter minds instead of soothing
them. The ordinary dead who were only ever passing through got caught in the
freeze, twisted into **Wraiths**, **Banshees**, **Poltergeists**, **Shades**
and worse, unable to finish crossing, unable to go back.

The Veil has had a new name ever since, given to it by the handful of living
who still trade at its edges: **the Hollow Veil.**

## Hollow Hamlet

A cluster of villagers once wandered through a natural crack between worlds
during a harvest storm and couldn't find their way back. Generations later
their descendants still live in **Hollow Hamlet**, a village of ashwood and
bonestone built defensively around a working Warden shrine — one of the only
safe, lit places left in the dimension. They have made an uneasy peace with
the Veil: they don't go out at Hollow Night, they leave offerings of salt and
silver at the boundary stones, and they trade with anyone brave enough to
bring back proof that the Wardens can still be reached.

The village's **Occultist** is the closest thing Hollow Hamlet has to a
leader-priest. They keep the shrine, remember the old names of the Wardens,
and are the player's main quest-giver and shop.

## The Quest Line (delivered via the in-game Journal item)

1. **Into the Grey** — Arrive in the Hollow Veil for the first time. Find
   Hollow Hamlet and speak with the Occultist.
2. **Proof of Passage** — Collect Soul Shards and Ember Dust from the Veil's
   wildlife to prove you can survive out there; unlocks basic trades.
3. **The Three Wardens** — The Occultist explains the corruption and marks
   the three boss chambers on the map: the **Sunken Crypt** (Hollow King),
   **Widow's Hollow** (Weeping Widow) and the **Cinder Bastion** (Malacoda).
4. **Crypt / Hollow / Bastion** (repeatable per-boss chapter) — Defeat a
   Warden. Each Warden's death releases a fragment of their old, uncorrupted
   self, which the Occultist can use to advance their trades and the
   village's shrine light radius (a small, visible reward: more lanterns
   light up in Hollow Hamlet after each boss).
5. **What Opened the Door** — After all three Wardens fall, the Occultist
   reveals what they've suspected the whole time: Malacoda didn't act alone,
   and the door is still ajar. This is deliberately left as a hook for a
   future update rather than a forced final boss, so the current story
   resolves (three real victories, a village restored) without overpromising
   content that doesn't exist yet.

## Tone Notes for Future Content

- The Veil is *sad*, not just spooky — the "monsters" are victims of the
  corruption, which is why defeating a boss releases a redeemed fragment
  instead of just loot.
- Hollow Hamlet should always feel like the one warm room in a cold house:
  lantern light, villager chatter, safety.
- Reserve full demonic imagery (fire, wings, horns) for Malacoda's Cinder
  Bastion specifically — everywhere else leans pale, grey and quiet so the
  Bastion's heat and color hit harder by contrast.
